Trust Network

How it works

This app proves one thing: that you are a real, living person — not a bot, not a duplicate account. It does that the old-fashioned way: other real people meet you face to face, look at your government ID, and vouch for you with their phone. The result is a public number, your Real Person Score, that websites, Discord servers and doors can check in seconds without learning anything else about you.

Your score, in one paragraph

0–100: how confident the network is that this is one real, living person. It goes up when trusted people check their ID face to face, counts people from different social circles far more than friends-of-friends, and slowly fades (half-life 90 days) unless they are re-verified.

Unverified
Nobody the network trusts has checked this person's ID face to face yet.
Provisional
Verified in person, but so far only by one group of connected people (or not strongly enough yet). A check from someone outside that group makes it Verified.
Verified
Checked in person, against government ID, by at least two independent groups of people.

Thresholds: Provisional from 30; Verified from 70 and at least 2 independent groups of verifiers.

Why can't my friends just all verify me?

They can, and it helps — but it saturates. How many separate groups of people have verified them. Ten mutual friends count as one group; one person from a different circle is worth more than nine more friends. Verified needs at least 2. This is what makes the score hard to fake: a bot farm can verify itself all day and still look like one group.

How do I get verified?

  1. Register on this phone (Home). That creates your pseudonymous Trust ID and a signing key that never leaves the device.
  2. Meet a certifier in person. A certifier is someone the network already trusts to check IDs — appointed by the two founders, and accountable for every check they make.
  3. Run the ceremony. Open Get verified in person, show them your code, then scan the rotating code on their phone. Both phones sign the same 30-second challenge from the same place, so nobody can be "verified" over a video call.
  4. They check your ID. The certifier compares your face to a government photo ID and records only the type of document and the issuing country. Your ID number, name and photo are never uploaded.
  5. Your score updates immediately and you receive an Identity Credential (valid 90 days, renewable while your score holds).

What do I do with it?

Whenever something wants proof you're human — a Discord server, a website, a door — it shows a request code. Open Present my score, scan it, and your phone signs the request. The service hands back a token (valid 2–5 minutes, single use) containing just your score snapshot; you show that as a QR code and the door decides. The door never learns who verified you. The Door tab in this app is a demo of exactly that.

Can I use it on my laptop too?

Yes. There is no password to log in with — your identity is the signing key in your device, and keys never leave the device they were made on. To add a laptop, open the app there and tap Link this device: it makes its own key and shows a code. On your phone, open My devices, scan the code and approve. From then on both devices act as you. Lose one? Remove it from any other device and it is locked out instantly. Linking devices never changes your score — the score belongs to you, not to a phone.

What's public and what's private?

  • Public: your username, your score, your status, how many independent groups verified you, and the date of your last check — your Trust Passport.
  • Private, always: who verified whom. The graph of checks exists only on the server; no page, token or API ever exposes it.
  • Never stored: ID numbers, document photos, your exact location (only a coarse ~1 km cell hash during a ceremony, to confirm both phones were together).

What if someone cheats?

A certifier who falsely declares an ID check can be reported. A panel of three other certifiers reviews it and both founders must sign off. If confirmed, the cheater's own trust is slashed — and because every score they contributed to is recomputed from the maths, a bot they verified drops to zero while a real person with other verifiers barely moves.

Glossary

Trust ID
A random, pseudonymous ID. The score attaches to it — never to your name — and it never reveals who verified you.
Device key
The signing key created on your phone at registration. It signs ceremony confirmations and presentation requests. Lose the phone, lose the key — recovery is a fresh in-person check.
Passkey
Face ID / fingerprint login for your account (optional). It is a login factor, not the signing key.
Certifier
A person appointed by a founder to run ceremonies and check IDs. Certifiers earn XP for useful checks (especially bridging separate groups) — XP never affects anyone's score.
Roots
The two founders of the network. All trust flows from them; destructive actions (slashing, removing a certifier) need both.
Assurance
How strong the ID check was: ID-verified (A certifier compared this person's face to a government photo ID in person.) or Root-vouched — no documentary ID (One of the two network founders personally vouched for this person without seeing an ID. It is labelled honestly so nobody mistakes it for an ID check.)
Independent clusters
How many separate groups of people have verified them. Ten mutual friends count as one group; one person from a different circle is worth more than nine more friends. Verified needs at least 2.
Last verified in person
The most recent in-person check. Older checks count for less, so people stay verified by being re-seen, not by having been seen once.
Presentation token
The short-lived, single-use proof your phone hands a door. It carries your score snapshot, countersigned by the service so it cannot be forged or inflated.
